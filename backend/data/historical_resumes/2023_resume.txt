Red Bull Racing
Max Verstappen (1):
Verstappen’s 2023 campaign was a masterclass in relentless precision. He proved nearly invincible when starting from the front, converting almost every pole position into a victory, such as in Bahrain, Spain, and Britain. His performance was not limited to front-running, however; his ability to carve through the field was evident in Miami, where he won from 9th on the grid. Verstappen excelled in variable weather, most notably at the Dutch Grand Prix, where he managed multiple tire changes and a late-race red flag to win in torrential rain. He displayed total dominance on high-speed circuits like Spa-Francorchamps and Monza, utilizing the RB19’s aerodynamic efficiency to maintain gaps that neutralized any DRS threat from rivals. With almost no DNFs caused by driver error, his season was defined by a level of consistency that rewritten the record books.

Sergio Perez (11):
Perez began the season as a "Street Circuit King," taking commanding victories in Saudi Arabia and Azerbaijan. On these low-grip, high-consequence tracks, his ability to manage rear-tire degradation while staying close to the walls was unmatched early on. However, his performance dipped significantly during the European leg. He struggled in wet/dry qualifying sessions, leading to a string of failures to reach Q3, most notably in Monaco and Britain. While he often recovered positions on Sunday, his race-day pace rarely matched Verstappen’s, especially on high-speed permanent tracks like Barcelona or Suzuka where aerodynamic stability is paramount. Despite these struggles, his ability to secure P2 in the championship was aided by strong podium finishes at the start and end of the season.

Mercedes-AMG Petronas
Lewis Hamilton (44):
Hamilton’s 2023 was defined by race-day consistency and an ability to extract maximum points from a car that often lacked qualifying pace. He was particularly strong on technical, high-downforce circuits like Hungary, where he took a surprise pole position, and Mexico City, where he showed elite tire management to secure P2. Hamilton’s performance in the rain remained a high point, as seen during his charge in the mixed conditions of Zandvoort. While he lacked the straight-line speed to challenge the Red Bulls on high-speed straights, his late-braking capability into low-speed turns kept him at the sharp end of the points.

George Russell (63):
Russell showed exceptional qualifying speed, often matching or outperforming Hamilton on Saturdays, such as in Australia where he started on the front row. However, his season was marred by a few high-profile DNFs and errors, most notably a last-lap crash in Singapore while fighting for the win. He excelled on high-speed, flowing tracks like Silverstone and Spain, where his aggressive driving style suited the Mercedes' aerodynamic platform. While he suffered from some bad luck in variable weather conditions, his overall pace remained elite, even if the final points tally didn't always reflect his speed.

Scuderia Ferrari
Charles Leclerc (16):
Leclerc remained the standout qualifier of the field, taking poles in Azerbaijan, Belgium, Austin, Mexico, and Las Vegas. He was untouchable on street circuits and tracks with long straights followed by heavy braking zones, utilizing the Ferrari's impressive "short-burst" acceleration. However, Leclerc suffered from several DNFs, including a mechanical failure on the formation lap in Brazil and a crash in Australia. His performance in wet weather was inconsistent compared to his rivals, often struggling with the Ferrari’s narrow operating window in changing grip levels.

Carlos Sainz (55):
Sainz delivered the only non-Red Bull win of the season in Singapore, a testament to his tactical intelligence and composure under pressure on a high-stakes street circuit. He was notably strong in the second half of the season, taking consecutive poles in Monza and Singapore. Sainz tended to perform better than Leclerc on high-speed circuits where a more stable, understeering car balance was preferred, such as Monza. While he DNF'd in Belgium following a first-lap collision, his ability to "think" his way through a race—managing DRS trains to defend his position—made him one of the most effective defensive drivers of 2023.

Aston Martin Aramco
Fernando Alonso (14):
Alonso was the story of the first half of the season, scoring six podiums in the first eight races. He was a master of the low-speed turns and heavy braking zones of Bahrain, Monaco, and Canada. His racecraft in variable weather was legendary, particularly his overtakes at the start of the rain-hit Dutch GP. As the team fell back in the development race, Alonso still managed to secure a heroic podium in Brazil, winning a high-speed drag race to the line against Sergio Perez. He rarely made mistakes and finished nearly every race, maximizing the car’s potential regardless of the circuit type.

Lance Stroll (18):
Stroll’s season began with a heroic recovery from broken wrists to finish P6 in Bahrain. However, he struggled to match Alonso’s consistency, particularly in qualifying. He was often vulnerable on high-speed circuits where the car’s aerodynamic limitations were exposed. His best performances came on tracks with high grip and physical demand, such as Brazil, where he secured a strong P5. He suffered several DNFs and mid-season slumps, but his ability to score points in the final flyaway races helped Aston Martin stay in the hunt for the top four.

McLaren
Lando Norris (4):
Norris’s season was a tale of two halves. Following a massive upgrade in Austria, he became Verstappen’s most consistent challenger. He excelled on high-speed, high-downforce tracks like Silverstone, Suzuka, and Qatar, where the McLaren MCL60 was arguably the second-fastest car. Norris showed great composure in the wet during the early stages of the British GP and the Dutch GP. Although he missed out on a win, his string of P2 finishes proved he is ready to compete at the very front when provided with the machinery.

Oscar Piastri (81):
In one of the greatest rookie seasons in modern history, Piastri took a Sprint race win in Qatar, out-dueling Verstappen in high-speed, high-deg conditions. He was exceptionally quick on high-turn circuits that required high aerodynamic confidence, like Suzuka and Spa. While he initially struggled with tire management in full-length races compared to Norris, his growth was evident by the end of the year. He suffered a few DNFs due to opening-lap incidents, such as in Belgium and Hungary, but his qualifying pace often put him in the top five.

Alpine
Pierre Gasly (10):
Gasly adapted well to his new team, highlighting his season with a podium in the chaotic, rain-hit Dutch Grand Prix. He was strong on power-sensitive tracks and showed great defensive skills in the mid-field. His season was marked by a fierce internal rivalry with Ocon, which occasionally boiled over, most notably in the late-race restart in Australia where both cars retired after a collision.

Esteban Ocon (31):
Ocon’s season highlight was a brilliant podium in Monaco, where he defended flawlessly against faster cars on the tight street circuit. However, he suffered the most DNFs of any driver in 2023 (seven total), many of which were due to mechanical unreliability or being caught in mid-field skirmishes. He remained a specialist in high-turn, low-speed sections but struggled to find consistency on the high-speed permanent circuits.

Williams
Alexander Albon (23):
Albon was arguably the "pound-for-pound" driver of the season. The Williams was a "rocket ship" in a straight line, and Albon used this to perfection to defend P7 in Canada and Monza against much faster cars. He was a frequent visitor to Q3, proving his speed on low-downforce, high-speed tracks. While the car struggled in high-downforce turns and wet weather (with the exception of Zandvoort qualifying), Albon’s ability to "park the bus" and manage tires under pressure earned Williams P7 in the standings.

Logan Sargeant (2):
A difficult rookie year for Sargeant, who struggled with high-speed crashes and qualifying pace. His highlight was scoring a point at his home race in Austin after disqualifications ahead of him. He showed flashes of speed on street circuits like Las Vegas but often suffered from DNFs when pushing the car past its limit.

Haas F1 Team
Nico Hulkenberg (27):
Hulkenberg was a qualifying sensation, frequently putting the Haas into Q3 and even securing a front-row start (later penalized) in the wet conditions of Canada. However, the Haas car suffered from extreme tire overheating, meaning he often plummeted down the order during races. He excelled on tracks that prioritized one-lap speed over long-run pace.

Kevin Magnussen (20):
Magnussen had a harder time than Hulkenberg in qualifying but was often more aggressive in the opening laps of races. His best result was a P10 in Miami, a track where he had previously shown strength. Like his teammate, his season was hampered by the car's inability to maintain pace on high-speed circuits.

Alfa Romeo / AlphaTauri / Analysis of Others
Yuki Tsunoda (22):
Tsunoda stepped up as a leader for AlphaTauri, especially after the car received late-season upgrades. He was brilliant in the season finale in Abu Dhabi, finishing P8 after a daring one-stop strategy.

Daniel Ricciardo (3):
Returning mid-season to replace Nyck de Vries, Ricciardo showed his class in Mexico, qualifying P4 and finishing P7. A hand injury in Zandvoort sidelined him for several races, but his presence brought much-needed stability to the team.

Liam Lawson (40):
Lawson impressed everyone as a mid-season substitute, scoring points in Singapore (one of the toughest races on the calendar) and showing incredible composure in the wet conditions of Zandvoort.

Valtteri Bottas (77) & Zhou Guanyu (24):
Both Alfa Romeo drivers struggled with a car that lacked a clear aerodynamic identity. Zhou was impressive in the opening lap of Spain, while Bottas used his experience to grab points in Qatar and Mexico.

Nyck de Vries (21):
De Vries struggled to adapt to the AlphaTauri and was replaced after ten races, having failed to score points in a car that was, at the time, the slowest on the grid.
