Red Bull Racing
Max Verstappen (#1): Verstappen’s 2025 campaign was defined by strategic brilliance and high-speed mastery, though he lacked the absolute dominance of previous years. He secured pole positions on high-speed layouts like Suzuka, Jeddah, Miami, and Silverstone, demonstrating that he remains the benchmark on permanent, flowing tracks. He was clinical in Japan and Emilia Romagna, converting front-row starts into dominant victories. However, he showed relative vulnerability on low-speed street circuits and stop-start tracks; he qualified P7 in Bahrain and P5 in Monaco, where the RB21 seemed to struggle with bumps and slow-speed rotation. His reliability was generally strong, with his only major race retirement occurring in Austria. Notably, while he remained a force in qualifying, he often had to fight harder in races to maintain position against a surging McLaren.

Liam Lawson (#30): Lawson faced a challenging season as a rookie at Red Bull, struggling to find consistency in a car that appeared sensitive to setup. His qualifying performance was a significant weakness, often starting from the back of the grid (P18-P20). Despite this, he showed flashes of potential on street circuits, specifically in Monaco where he navigated the chaos to secure a season-best P8 finish. His year was marred by high-pressure errors and reliability woes, suffering DNFs in Australia, Miami, and Britain. He performed better on tracks with high traction demands but struggled significantly to find pace on high-speed permanent circuits compared to his teammate.

McLaren
Oscar Piastri (#81): Piastri was arguably the standout performer of 2025, demonstrating remarkable composure and technical precision. He dominated the middle part of the season, taking victories in China, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Miami, and Spain. His qualifying was elite, securing pole positions in China, Bahrain, Emilia Romagna, and Spain. Piastri excelled particularly on technical tracks with high-speed turns and street hybrids, showing a unique ability to manage tires while maintaining a qualifying-level pace during race stints. His only major setback was a DNF in the Miami Sprint, but he remained remarkably consistent elsewhere, rarely finishing outside the top four.

Lando Norris (#4): Norris proved himself a master of specific track archetypes, particularly high-speed layouts and tight street circuits. He secured a statement win from pole in Australia and delivered a masterclass on the narrow streets of Monaco to take another victory. He also dominated the latter half of the European leg, winning in both Austria and Britain. Norris was exceptionally fast in qualifying, taking poles in Australia, Monaco, and Austria. While he suffered a heartbreaking race DNF in Canada and a qualifying failure in Saudi Arabia, his ability to extract performance in variable conditions and high-speed sections made him a constant threat for the championship.

Ferrari
Charles Leclerc (#16): Leclerc maintained his reputation as one of the fastest drivers over a single lap, though race results were often dictated by technical luck. He was a specialist on street circuits, qualifying P2 in Monaco and Saudi Arabia, and converting those into podium finishes. His performance on high-degradation tracks like Spain and Bahrain remained strong, yielding P3 and P4 finishes respectively. However, his season was hampered by a disqualification in the Chinese Grand Prix and a difficult outing in Britain where he finished P14. He typically performed best on tracks requiring precision and low-speed agility rather than raw top-end speed.

Lewis Hamilton (#44): In his first full season with Ferrari, Hamilton focused on race management and maximizing points in difficult scenarios. While he lacked the qualifying edge of his teammate, often starting mid-pack, his race pace remained world-class. He secured season-high P4 finishes in Emilia Romagna and his home race in Britain, showing great strength on high-speed, technical tracks. He struggled early on with the car's balance in variable conditions, suffering a DNF in the China Sprint and a race disqualification in China. Despite these setbacks, he was highly consistent in the points, specializing in recovering positions on circuits with long straights and heavy braking zones.

Mercedes
George Russell (#63): Russell was the qualifying leader for Mercedes, frequently placing the car in the top three. His crowning achievement was a dominant pole-to-win performance at the Canadian Grand Prix, a track that rewarded his aggressive braking and mastery of kerbs. He was a constant podium threat, finishing P3 in Australia, China, and Miami, and P2 in Bahrain. Russell excelled on high-speed permanent tracks and stop-start layouts alike, showing improved maturity in race combat. His consistency was only broken by a minor points finish in Britain following a difficult qualifying session.

Kimi Antonelli (#12): Antonelli’s debut season was a rollercoaster of raw speed and rookie mistakes. He proved his potential early with a P4 in Australia and a P3 in Saudi Arabia, showcasing incredible bravery on high-speed street tracks. He was also a sensation in Sprint Qualifying, taking pole in Miami. However, his aggressive style led to numerous retirements, with DNFs in Emilia Romagna, Spain, Austria, and Britain. He showed a preference for tracks with high-speed turns but struggled with consistency in the more technical, low-speed sections of tracks like Monaco, where he finished near the back.

Aston Martin
Fernando Alonso (#14): Alonso maximized the Aston Martin package early in the season, though the car's development seemed to plateau. He was highly effective on technical tracks and stop-start circuits, with his best race result being a P7 in Canada. He remained one of the most reliable finishers on the grid, though he suffered uncharacteristic DNFs in Australia, China, and Monaco. His qualifying was solid, often reaching Q3, but he found it increasingly difficult to defend against the top four teams on high-speed layouts as the year progressed.

Lance Stroll (#18): Stroll had a mixed 2025, with his best performance coming at the season opener in Australia, where he finished P6. He generally performed better on tracks with long straights and heavy braking, but often struggled to match the raw qualifying speed of the front-runners. His season was characterized by middle-of-the-pack finishes, though he remained a reliable pair of hands in chaotic race starts, avoiding the DNFs that affected many of his midfield rivals.

Racing Bulls (VCARB)
Yuki Tsunoda (#22): Tsunoda continued his evolution into a top-tier midfield operator. He was particularly strong in qualifying, notably taking P5 in Australia. His race management saw a significant step up, highlighted by a P6 finish in Miami. He excelled on technical, high-turn circuits but faced setbacks with race retirements in Saudi Arabia and Emilia Romagna. Despite these DNFs, he was often the class of the midfield on street-hybrid tracks.

Isack Hadjar (#6): Hadjar had an impressive rookie campaign, showing a particular affinity for high-speed permanent tracks. His standout result was a P5 finish in Spain, where he outperformed several faster cars. He also displayed great composure on the streets of Monaco, finishing P6. While he struggled in the early stop-start races like Bahrain, his growth throughout the European leg was evident, showing strong pace in high-speed sections.

Alpine
Pierre Gasly (#10): Gasly led the Alpine team through a turbulent year. His best outing was a P6 in Bahrain, where he maximized the car's traction in heavy braking zones. He was strong in managing technical sections but suffered from the car's lack of top-end speed on tracks like Monza or Spa. His season included DNFs in Saudi Arabia and Monaco, as well as a disqualification in the Chinese race.

Jack Doohan (#7): Doohan struggled to make an impact in a car that often lacked the pace to compete for points. His season was hampered by early retirements in Australia and Miami. He showed some qualifying promise on high-speed layouts but frequently fell back during race stints due to high tire degradation.

Franco Colapinto (#43): Appearing in several rounds for Alpine, Colapinto showed solid defensive skills. His best finish was P12 in the high-speed Saudi Arabian GP. He was generally reliable in race conditions, though he suffered a DNS (Did Not Start) at the British Grand Prix.

Haas
Esteban Ocon (#31): Ocon provided Haas with veteran consistency. He delivered a brilliant P5 in the Chinese Grand Prix, proving his strength on technical layouts with long straights. While he suffered a DNF in Emilia Romagna, he was a frequent points-scorer on high-speed tracks, utilizing his defensive prowess to hold off faster cars.

Oliver Bearman (#38/87): Bearman (racing under #87 in several sessions) was a consistent performer for Haas. He scored points in China (P8), Japan (P10), and Bahrain (P10). He showed a high level of adaptability, performing well on both the high-speed sweeps of Suzuka and the technical demands of Shanghai. His ability to stay out of trouble in mid-field scraps was a hallmark of his season.

Williams
Alexander Albon (#23): Albon remained the spearhead for Williams, delivering exceptional performances on tracks that suited the car's aerodynamic efficiency. He secured P5 finishes in Australia, Miami, and Emilia Romagna, showcasing his skill on technical street hybrids and old-school tracks. However, he faced a difficult stretch mid-season with DNFs in Canada and Austria. He was a specialist at qualifying the car higher than its race pace would suggest.

Carlos Sainz (#55): Sainz’s first year with Williams was marked by flashes of brilliance interrupted by reliability issues. He secured strong P8 finishes in Saudi Arabia and Emilia Romagna. However, his season was marred by DNFs in Australia and Bahrain, and a DNS in Austria. He typically excelled on technical circuits with medium-to-high speed turns but struggled to find consistency on the most power-sensitive tracks.

Kick Sauber
Nico Hulkenberg (#27): Hulkenberg was the qualifying sensation of the midfield, but his race results were even more impressive in 2025. He secured a phenomenal P3 podium finish at the high-speed British Grand Prix and a P5 in Spain. He was exceptionally fast on high-turn circuits, though he suffered a disqualification in Bahrain. He was one of the most consistent drivers in extracting 100% from the car's potential.

Gabriel Bortoleto (#5): Bortoleto’s rookie year was a steep learning curve. He showed flashes of pace on technical tracks, finishing P12 in both China and Spain. However, he struggled on street circuits, suffering DNFs in both Australia and Miami. He generally performed better on permanent circuits where he could find a rhythm in high-speed corners.
