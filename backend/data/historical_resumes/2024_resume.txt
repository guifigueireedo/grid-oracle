Red Bull Racing
Max Verstappen (#1): Verstappen began the year with total qualifying dominance, securing pole positions in the first seven rounds. He is nearly clinical when starting from the front, converting poles in Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Japan, China, and Emilia Romagna into race wins. While his high-speed performance on permanent tracks like Suzuka and Silverstone remains the benchmark, he showed relative vulnerability on low-speed street circuits with bumps and kerbs, notably qualifying P6 in Monaco. His only DNF was a rare mechanical brake failure in Australia.

Sergio Perez (#11): Perez started the season strongly with several P2 finishes behind his teammate, showing good pace on high-speed layouts like Jeddah. However, his form dipped significantly as the season moved into Europe. He struggled immensely in qualifying, suffering Q1 exits in Monaco, Canada, and Britain. His performance on street circuits, usually a strength, was marred by a heavy DNF in Monaco after a first-lap collision. He tends to perform better on tracks with high-traction demands but has struggled with consistency in variable weather.

McLaren
Lando Norris (#4): Norris evolved into Verstappen's primary challenger, particularly after the Miami upgrades. He secured a breakthrough win in Miami and a pole position in Spain. He is exceptionally fast on high-speed, high-turn tracks like Barcelona and Silverstone but has faced challenges in maintaining the lead on Lap 1 when starting from the front. Norris has proven to be a master of variable conditions, showing great pace during the wet-to-dry transitions in Canada and Britain.

Oscar Piastri (#81): Piastri has demonstrated remarkable composure, often matching Norris’s qualifying pace. He was particularly strong on the tight Monaco street circuit, qualifying and finishing P2. While he is very consistent and rarely DNFs (only one DNF in the Miami Sprint), his race pace sometimes falls slightly behind Norris on high-degradation permanent circuits. He excels in high-speed sections, as evidenced by his podium in Austria.

Ferrari
Charles Leclerc (#16): Leclerc remains one of the grid's elite qualifiers, culminating in a dominant pole and win on his home street circuit in Monaco. He is highly effective when starting from the front, though his season became inconsistent following Monaco. He suffered a DNF in the chaotic Canadian GP due to engine issues and struggled on the high-speed layouts of Austria and Britain. He typically performs best on tracks requiring precision and low-speed agility.

Carlos Sainz (#55): Sainz’s 2024 is defined by his resilience, winning the Australian Grand Prix just weeks after surgery. He is arguably the most consistent driver on the grid, finishing in the top five in almost every race he started. Sainz excels on technical circuits with medium-to-high speed turns and is very reliable in chaotic race starts, avoiding the DNFs that plagued his teammate mid-season.

Oliver Bearman (#38): In his single appearance in Saudi Arabia, Bearman showed incredible adaptability on one of the fastest and most dangerous street circuits, qualifying P11 and finishing P7 under immense pressure.

Mercedes
Lewis Hamilton (#44): Hamilton struggled with the car's balance early in the year but showed his legendary prowess as the Mercedes improved. He is peerless in wet and variable conditions, as demonstrated by his emotional victory at the high-speed Silverstone circuit. While his qualifying was initially inconsistent compared to his teammate, his race management on high-speed tracks remains world-class.

George Russell (#63): Russell has been Mercedes' qualifying leader for much of the year, taking poles in Canada and Britain. He is very aggressive and successful on high-speed permanent tracks, winning the Austrian GP after capitalizing on the lead duo's collision. However, he suffered a heartbreaking DNF from pole at his home race in Britain due to a water pressure issue.

Aston Martin
Fernando Alonso (#14): Alonso maximized a declining car early on, often qualifying in the top five. He is a specialist in extracting performance on street circuits like Saudi Arabia (P5) but has found it harder to maintain those positions on high-speed tracks as the car's development lagged. He remains highly consistent in finishing races, with only one DNF in the China Sprint.

Lance Stroll (#18): Stroll has had a mixed year but showed a distinct strength in wet and chaotic conditions, particularly in Canada where he secured a strong P7 finish. He generally performs better on tracks with long straights and heavy braking zones but often struggles to match Alonso's raw qualifying speed.

Yuki Tsunoda (#22): Tsunoda has been one of the standout performers of the midfield, frequently reaching Q3. He is particularly strong on technical, high-turn circuits and street hybrids, securing P7 finishes in both Australia and Miami. His DNF in China was a setback, but he has generally shown much-improved maturity and consistency.

Daniel Ricciardo (#3): Ricciardo's season was slow to start, but he showed flashes of his old self on street circuits, specifically in the Miami Sprint where he qualified P4 and defended brilliantly to finish P4. He has struggled more on high-speed permanent tracks compared to Tsunoda.

Haas
Nico Hulkenberg (#27): Hulkenberg has been a qualifying sensation, regularly placing the Haas in the top 10. He is exceptionally strong on high-speed tracks, as shown by back-to-back P6 finishes in Austria and Britain. He rarely makes mistakes, though the car's race pace sometimes fails him on high-turn circuits.

Kevin Magnussen (#20): Magnussen has played a vital team role, often defending aggressively to help Hulkenberg score points. He excels in high-speed battles but has been prone to penalties and was involved in the major Monaco DNF.

Alpine
Pierre Gasly (#10): After a disastrous start for Alpine, Gasly found consistency as the car improved, scoring points in four consecutive races from Canada to Britain. He is strong in managing difficult conditions and technical tracks.

Esteban Ocon (#31): Ocon has been evenly matched with Gasly but suffered from internal team friction, most notably a DNF in Monaco after a collision with his teammate. He performed well in the variable conditions of Canada and Spain.

Williams
Alexander Albon (#23): Albon continues to carry Williams, showing immense strength in qualifying on tracks like Monaco and Silverstone. He is a specialist at defending on tracks with long straights. He suffered DNFs in Japan and Canada, both involving collisions.

Logan Sargeant (#2): Sargeant struggled throughout the first half of the season, often qualifying at the back of the grid. He suffered a DNF in Miami and has generally lacked the pace to compete for points on either street or permanent circuits.

Sauber
Valtteri Bottas (#77): Bottas has often outperformed the Sauber car in qualifying, especially on technical tracks like China. However, poor pit stops and lack of race pace have prevented him from scoring points. He has remained remarkably consistent in finishing races, with only one DNF in China.

Zhou Guanyu (#24): Zhou had a strong start in Bahrain (P11) but has struggled since. He is generally safe but lacks the peak qualifying speed of Bottas. He had a DNF in the Japanese GP due to a gearbox issue.
